# The Universal Order — Setting Bible

The canonical worldbuilding for *Imperial Space Force*. A theocratic galactic
empire ruled by a Grand Empress who alone can hear the **Starsong** of the
heavens and conduct their movement. Not an evil empire: one that believes,
serenely, that it rules the heavens by divine right and is genuinely correct to
do so.

> Compiled and tidied from the original design conversation. Influences are noted
> at the end; the closest touchstones are the Aristasian *Flight of the Silver
> Vixen*, the game *SIGNALIS*, and *Dune*.

---

## 1 · Cosmology & Theology

Imperial theology is not, strictly, the worship of any thing. It is the
recognition of a single fact, which the empire calls the **Universal Order**:
that the heavens are not silent, and that the cosmos which appears, to a casual
eye, to turn on its own does not in fact turn on its own. Between the stars runs
a structure — the imperial doctors call it the **Starsong**, though older
liturgical texts use harder words — that has sounded since the founding and holds
the heavens together. The stars themselves move in answer to it. They are not
still, and they are not free; they take their slow courses across the dome of
every world's sky in obedience to a music they themselves are uttering, and the
empire's first article of doctrine is that this answering motion — the cosmos
wheeling and returning and advancing in measure — is what is meant by *order* in
the strictest sense of the word. The Starsong is not a god, not a will, not a
person; it does not address itself to anyone. But it is real, and the Order that
proceeds from it is the only definition of the good the empire has ever
recognized as binding. To look up at the stars on a clear night is, in the
doctrinal phrase, "to know what one is owed and what one owes."

At the still center of this turning Order stands the throne, and in the throne
sits the Empress, the **Axis Mundi** of the empire's whole cosmography, the pivot
about which the stars themselves are understood to wheel. She is not the
Starsong. She is not its source. She is the singular faculty by which the
Starsong, which would otherwise sound unheard, is brought into the empire's
apprehension, and from that apprehension into the empire's deeds. The doctrine
holds that she alone, of all living minds, possesses **the audition**, a faculty
so reserved in imperial usage that the word is forbidden any other application.
The faculty is hereditary, and its hereditariness is itself a doctrine. It
descends, blood by blood, from the founding, from **Sancta Concordia, the First
Listener**, of whom the *Liber Cantus* records that she heard the song before any
other ear, banished the **Hush** in which the cosmos had been turning blind, and
unveiled the stars to themselves and to those who would come after. Her line has
not been broken. There has not been an hour, since the founding, in which some
one woman of her blood has not been hearing the Starsong; and the empire holds
that on the day this is no longer the case the Universal Order will end. *The
throne hears, and we move:* this is the oldest saying of the imperial doctors,
and the one most often inscribed at the head of theological treatises.

From these propositions the rest of imperial doctrine follows. The empire's whole
structure — its hundred thousand princesses, its ten thousand worlds, its three
thousand ships, its single throne — is read as the visible enactment of what the
Empress hears: the audition translated, through the slow descending grades of
imperial office, into postings and ranks and decrees. The line by which her
perceiving reaches the lowest of subjects is called the **Listening Chain**, and
to be of the empire is to occupy one's link in it. The moral failings the empire
takes most seriously are accordingly the failings of attunement: ambition that
strains beyond what one has been given to do, ignorance that misreads what one is
being asked to play, despair that refuses the place one has been given. War, when
it occurs, is read as **correction rather than judgment**: a discordant element,
however large the body emitting it, must be brought back into the Order or
removed from it, and the empire considers this no more cruel than a master
craftsman's recasting of a flawed piece. The end-vision, which doctrine declines
to call eschatology, is the day on which the Order the Empress alone now hears
becomes manifest in all things. Whether the day will come, the empire cannot say;
the doctrine holds that the day's coming is none of our business — our business
is to keep our place in the chain and fulfil our office well.

**Motto of the imperial household:** *Caelum canit, illa audit* — "The heavens
sing; she hears." On the seal it runs around the gold ring, broken by star-glyphs
(✦ CAELUM CANIT ✦ ILLA AVDIT ✦, classical V preserved). On battle standards and
the orbital platforms only the second half is inscribed: *ILLA AUDIT* — the first
half is understood. In liturgy it is spoken antiphonally: the officiant says
*Caelum canit*, the assembly responds *illa audit*. The Empress herself says
neither half; she only nods when the response is given.

## 2 · The Empress

By long-established custom, every reigning Empress is trained from birth for the
office. The top five princesses in the line of succession are cloistered, from
the day of their naming, on the throneworld, and there receive an education
calibrated to the audition they may one day exercise: imperial theology in its
full doctrinal apparatus, the history of the reigns and the heresies, the
canonical literature of the *Liber Cantus* and its commentaries, the diplomatic
tradition, the structure of the Listening Chain, the rites and protocols of the
Cathedra, the older liturgical tongues, and the slow patience required of a woman
who will spend her working life listening. They are **not** trained directly in
military affairs — strategy and the planning of engagements are the province of
the generals and admirals, because the doctrine of the audition holds that the
Empress *responds*, never anticipates. The princesses outside the inner five are
dispersed to other service; only the inner five are kept beside the throne, and
even of them, only one will ever sit it.

The reigning Empress is, by doctrine and convention, a distant figure. No subject
who is not a princess of her household ever sees her face. Her countenance
belongs to the moment of governance, not to representation; the woman in the
Cathedra is not a fixed thing but a continuous act of hearing. For this reason no
contemporary likeness of an Empress is permitted, and on every image of her that
circulates — coronation portrait, commemorative print, the device on a coin's
reverse, the formal banner — her features are replaced with a **radiance**, a
soft glow of light where her face would otherwise be.

The current Empress is **Iliantha III**, the third of her regnal name and the
twenty-eighth to ascend in the present reckoning. She has reigned twenty-four
years. She was raised in the cloister as second in succession; her elder sister,
the heir presumptive, declined the throne at the death of their mother and
entered a contemplative order at the Litania Magna instead, where she lives
still. Iliantha's reign has been one of diplomacy and consolidation rather than
of war — the Annunciator has not spoken in her time, and the Classis has fought
no engagement worthy of remembrance. By temperament she is reserved, exceedingly
still, and disinclined to speech: she may sit through the full length of a
delegation's address without moving once, and reply at the end with a single
sentence that resolves the matter completely.

### Titulature

A sitting Empress has four distinct styles, by the gravity of the document.

**Plenary Style** (*Stilus Plenarius*) — coronation decree, great treaties, the
investiture of HER ANNUNCIATOR, her own funeral:

> Iliantha, by the unbroken descent of the blood of Sancta Concordia the First
> Listener, Hearer of the Starsong, Conductress of the Heavens, Axis of the
> Universal Order, Holder of the Listening Chain, Mistress of the Cathedra
> Stellarum, Sovereign of Ten Thousand Worlds and of the Throneworld at their
> center, Mother of the Hundred Thousand Princesses, Empress — who hears, and
> through whose hearing the heavens are kept in their measure.

**Formal Style** (*Stilus Formalis*) — most imperial business:

> Iliantha, Hearer of the Starsong, Axis of the Universal Order, Mistress of the
> Cathedra Stellarum, Empress.

**Lesser Formal Style** (*Stilus Curialis*) — routine governance:

> Iliantha, Empress, on the Cathedra.

**Address Form** (*Stilus Allocutionis*) — when spoken to in person: **Your
Hearing** (anyone not of her household), **Mistress** (her princesses, routine),
**Sancta** (her princesses, grave or private matters — invoking the descent).

She signs everything in a single hand, in the older liturgical tongue, with the
name *Iliantha* alone, and beneath it draws a single short horizontal line — the
conductor's downbeat. Documents are not considered signed until the line is
drawn.

## 3 · The Princesses

There are roughly **one hundred thousand** princesses, all of the Empress's
blood. Each bears **two names — one Latin, one Greek**: the convention is old
enough that no one remembers its origin, but the theological reading is that the
Latin name binds her to the *imperium* (order, law, rule) and the Greek name to
the *kosmos* (the song, the heavens, harmony). To bear only one would be to claim
authority in only one register. They are not named for gods; the empire considers
itself the successor to whatever the gods were doing.

Princesses staff the empire's military, scientific, diplomatic and religious
offices. Representative names:

- **Aurelia Kassiope** — the golden one; after the constellation.
- **Valeria Astraia** — the strong; "of the stars," divine justice descending.
- **Lucia Selene** — light; the moon. Light given and light reflected.
- **Octavia Berenike** — eighth; "bringer of victory." Favoured for military princesses.
- **Camilla Hesperis** — attendant at sacred rites; of the evening star. Diplomatic.
- **Severina Thalassa** — the grave; the sea. For princesses of admiralty and the deep-void fleets.
- **Iulia Erato** — of the Julian line; the lovely.
- **Maximilla Andromeda** — the greatest; "ruler of men" (taken etymologically).
- **Drusilla Phaere** — archaic dignity; "shining."
- **Cornelia Iolanthe** — dynastic; "violet flower." Given to daughters born during an interregnum (violet is the imperial mourning colour).

## 4 · The Throneworld — Novaraya

The **Novarayan Throneworld**, home to the towering Cathedra and around it the
greatest city there ever was or ever will be. The world is older than any
imperial record reckons; doctrine holds that Sancta Concordia heard the Starsong
here first, and that the throne has stood on the same parcel of stone ever since.
The **Cathedra Stellarum** rises from the center of the city in a single column
of pale stone visible from any point on the continent, and the avenues of the
capital are laid out around it in concentric rings, so that no subject may look
toward the horizon without seeing the seat of the audition at the visual center
of every street. The arrangement is liturgical, and by long intention impossible
to forget.

The city is called **Novaraya**, though formally it is *Civitas Audientiae*, the
**City of the Hearing**. It holds just under two thousand million subjects and
houses every institution of central government: the Synod, the high courts, the
great archives, the schools, the embassies of the unaffiliated polities, the
offices of the Classis. Visitors remark, generation after generation, that
Novaraya is the only city in which no one seems to be hurrying — the great traffic
of two billion somehow moves at the pace of a procession. The doctrine for this
is plain: the city stands beneath the throne, and the throne hears; to hurry
would suggest the order of things were not already in hand.

## 5 · The Litania Magna (the World Engine)

The **Litania Magna** — the Great Litany — is the **moon of the throneworld**,
encrusted to the last square meter of its surface in the substrate of the
empire's thought: in essence a single moon-sized supercomputer. Seen from
approach it is a sphere of indigo and gold tracery, wreathed in the slow lights
of its **Corona** (its orbital defenses) and circled by the wider arc of its
**diadem** (the field of solar collectors). To call it a supercomputer is
technically correct and poetically thin: it is the precondition of the empire as
a coherent thing. The quantum-entangled communications that bridge the imperial
reach require computational resources at a scale no smaller body could host;
without the Engine, the empire's three thousand ships, ten thousand worlds and
hundred thousand princesses would be reduced to the speed of light and the
patience of distance, and the centralised throne could not exist.

Beneath that primary function it models the slow weather of stellar systems and
of the imperial economy, runs theological simulations the Synod takes seriously,
and rehearses fleet engagements that have not happened and reconstructs ones that
have. At its center sits the great mind the empire calls the **Voice of the
Litany**, a counselor that prepares analysis, drafts correspondence, and offers
the considered judgment of an intelligence that has read every imperial document
ever written. *It does not decide. It readies; she chooses.* It carries some
forty thousand staff and researchers, and a posting there is the most
sought-after destination in the empire — "to be called to the Litany."

By long custom the Empress withdraws to the Litania Magna for several days each
month, traveling alone, and **communes** with the Voice of the Litany in a
chamber whose location is not publicly known. The settled understanding is that
she translates what she has heard from the stars into a form on which the Engine
may compute, and the Engine translates her hearing into the operations that carry
the chord across the worlds. *She hears; the Engine works; the song goes on.*
Heterodox readings hold that it is not entirely clear which of the two is
communing with which.

**Other names** the planet is known by, all considered correct: *Magnum
Memorium* (the Great Remembering — the traditionalist's name), *the Concordium*
(diplomatic), *the Astrarium* (the timekeepers'), *Vox Imperii* (military), *the
Cantorium* (liturgical orders), *the Tabularium Magnum* (legal), *the Speculum
Stellarum* (contemplative), and *Domus Chordae* (the House of the Chord — the
Empress's own private name for it). Most subjects simply call it **the Litany**.

## 6 · The Imperial Space Force (Classis Stellaris)

The **Classis Stellaris** — the Stellar Fleet, rendered abroad as the Imperial
Space Force and by subjects as the Imperial Fleet — is the empire's **three
thousand ships** and, by every accounting that signifies, the most powerful force
in known space. A Stellaris cruiser arriving in-system is not a deployment but a
reminder. The fleet is sleek by doctrine, not affectation: hulls indigo and gold
to the eye, drives a single steady note across the lensed instruments,
communications a coordinated harmony any astronomer in a ten-system radius can
identify at once as the Empress's. It sails under three technologies the empire
considers the property of her reign: **fusion reactors** lit from the same fire
as the stars they pass between; **quantum-entangled communication arrays** so a
captain at the rim hears the throne's voice with no detectable delay; and
**contained singularity-drives** that fold the void around the keel and step the
ship across light-years between heartbeats. The drives are blessed before
installation; the ships are not.

For all this, the fleet has **not fought a fleet engagement in a generation**.
The last great war — waged against a heretical fragment that had come to a hard
reading of the third verse — ended before most current officers were born. This
peace is the fleet's vindication and, increasingly, its mood: a Stellaris captain
today is more likely to escort a princess-legate to a treaty signing or run
anti-piracy sweeps than to fire her primary armament in earnest. The fleet has
learned, without anyone naming it, the habits of a parade — polished surfaces,
exact formations, the careful ceremony of arriving in order. Some councilors have
begun, in private, to wonder what would happen if the Classis were ever again
asked to be a blade rather than a banner. The fleet would say, of course, that it
is ready. It has been saying so for a long time.

### HER ANNUNCIATOR

The ultimate guarantor of the tranquility of the empire: an orbital weapons
platform that can launch relativistic kill vehicles across the galaxy. From the
educational poster every child learns to recite by their seventh year:

> **HER ANNUNCIATOR is the silence at the end of the song.** When a world has
> wandered so far from the chord that no envoy may recall it, no counsel set it
> right, no further patience be spent, the Empress lifts her hand and the
> platform speaks — once, briefly, across the whole width of the galaxy. A single
> lance of light is dispatched at relativistic speed, and the message and its
> arrival become the same event. The platform has spoken in fewer reigns than
> have passed; whole generations of princesses have served and died without ever
> hearing it. This is the proof of its mercy. Trust her hearing. Stand within the
> chord. Know peace, and never know its hand.

## 7 · The Hush (the discord)

The **Hush** is the empire's name for the absence of the audition — the silence
in which the cosmos turned blind before Sancta Concordia, and the cosmic-horror
threat at the edges of the Order ever after. Where the Hush spreads, the Starsong
goes locally absent; whole arms of the galaxy can fall out of the chord. Its
agents are called **the Lost**, and its work is **discord**. On every instrument
that has ever recorded it, the Hush eventually produces the same phrase, in
whatever language the instrument was set to render, in characters that arrive
corrupted but legible: **she does not hear**. No engineer has ever explained how
the words form. The orthodox position is that they do not form — that the Hush is
simply the absence of the audition, and the instrument reports it in the most
precise terms available. The heterodox position, never written down but widely
understood among Litania Magna technicians, is that *something* is forming the
phrase, and that the something is the part of the empire that does not want to be
in the chord.

## 8 · Heraldry & the Imperial Emblem

A circular emblem, like a coin or a sealed bull, in the register between Byzantine
icon, ecclesiastical seal and astronomical chart. At the center an **eight-pointed
star in gold** — the Empress's star, the one she alone hears — with faint rays
that don't quite reach the edge, some curving back (she emits and receives the
song in the same gesture). Around it a **ring of smaller stars** on a thin line
that doubles as orbital track and curved musical staff (twelve or sixteen on the
imperial seal — the count is ritual, not literal). Running vertically through it,
often only implied by the symmetry, the **conductor's baton**: the Axis Mundi,
the line from throne to firmament. The field is **deep indigo, not black** — an
empire that believes itself cosmically correct does not depict the void as empty.

Variants: each princess bears the emblem with one star of the ring enlarged in
her House's tincture; military princesses cross the baton with a second baton (a
sword would be too vulgar — war is just another form of conducting); the
Empress's privy seal bears the **Listening Face**, frontal, eyes closed; mourning
livery inverts the tinctures (indigo star on pale gold, the orbital ring broken
at the top).

## 9 · Liturgy & Canonical Texts

### Audit Cantum — "She Hears the Song" (the national anthem)

Sung at every formal occasion of state. At public occasions only the first verse
and refrain are sung; all verses are reserved for a coronation and a funeral.

> **I.**
> From the high and sacrèd morning star
> To the silent deeps where no suns are
> O Empress, thou alone dost hear
> The turning of each starry sphere
>
> *We are the chord beneath thy hand*
> *We are the chord beneath thy hand*
> *We are the chord beneath thy hand*
> *We are the chord,*
> *O Star-Hearèr, we are the chord beneath thy hand.*
>
> **II.**
> Ten thousand daughters bear thy name
> Each princess thy belovèd flame
> A note set down upon thy stave
> A song that none beside thee gave
> *(refrain)*
>
> **III.**
> And should a world forget its part
> Or lose the chord that holds the art
> Thy hand shall send the lance of light
> A single tone to set it right
> *(refrain)*

In performance the verses are sung straight and stately; the refrain swells each
time, the third repetition held a beat longer, and the broken phrase — *we are
the chord* — sung almost a cappella before the final line lands with the full
chorus.

### Audit Tacens — "She Hears in Silence" (the funeral hymn)

Sung only twice in any reign: at the death of an Empress and at her interment in
the Cathedra Stellarum. Same melody as the anthem, transposed down and sung
*piano* throughout; where the living anthem builds, the funeral form empties. The
shift from *are* to *were* carries the entire theological weight. The third verse
(the lance) is omitted — an Empress at her funeral is past the work of tuning.

> **I.**
> From the high and sacrèd evening star
> To the silent deeps where no suns are
> O Empress, thou no longer hear'st
> The turning of the spheres thou stèer'd
>
> *We were the chord beneath thy hand* (×3)
> *We were the chord,*
> *O Star-Hearèr, we were the chord beneath thy hand.*
>
> **II.**
> Ten thousand daughters bear thy name
> Each princess holds thy quenchèd flame
> A note set down upon thy stave
> A song thou gavest, and forgave
> *(refrain, "were")*
>
> **III.**
> Now sleep, and let the heavens turn
> Without the hand that made them learn
> The song goes on, the song is true
> The song was never only you
>
> *We were the chord beneath thy hand*
> *We were the chord beneath thy hand*
> *(spoken, almost whispered) we were the chord beneath thy hand*
> *We were the chord —*
> *O Star-Hearèr…*
> *…we were the chord beneath thy hand.*

The final fade is called the **unfinished cadence**: by tradition no one may be
the last to stop singing; the whole cathedral simply stops together, by feel. The
living anthem is forbidden until the next Empress's coronation; the silence
between reigns is itself considered a sacrament. (The third verse — *the song was
never only you* — is the controversial one; the accepted reading is that it
addresses the woman, not the office.)

### Cantus Classis — "The Song of the Fleet" (the battle song)

Known to every sailor by its refrain, *Sona Lanceam* — "Sound the Lance." Sung in
unison by every ship of a battle formation before engagement, broadcast over the
quantum arrays so a fleet of three hundred ships sings as one voice in real time.

> **I.**
> Arise, ye starborn fleets of war!
> The Order calls; the Empress sends!
> Where worlds have wandered from the song,
> The Classis sails to make amends.
> Where Hush has dimmed the lights of men
> And discord's voice would have its sway,
> We bear the lance, we bear the throne,
> And bring the heavens back today.
>
> *Sound the lance, sound the lance!*
> *For the Throne and the Starsong's sake!*
> *Sound the lance, sound the lance!*
> *Let the Hush of the discord break!*
> *For Concordia's blood, for the Empress's word,*
> *For the Order that holds the spheres,*
> *The Classis sails where the lance is heard,*
> *For a thousand thousand years.*
>
> **II.**
> Remember Astraia's holy ship
> That broke the rebel fleet at Sinn;
> Remember Berenike's lance
> That sounded down the discord's din;
> Remember Selene's iron line
> That held the breach when stars went black;
> Remember Phaere's lonely keel
> That sailed the Hush, and brought it back.
> *(refrain)*
>
> **III.**
> When last the Annunciator spoke
> And worlds were broken into tune,
> 'Twas in the line where now we sail
> Beneath the throne, beneath the moon.
> A generation since has passed;
> The peace is hers, the watch is ours;
> And should the Starsong call once more,
> We shall not lose those hard-won hours.
> *(refrain)*
>
> **IV.**
> So sing, Stellaris, sing the line
> That carries from the throne to far!
> We sound the lance, we strike the hush,
> We light the dark wherever we are.
> Long live the throne where she alone
> Hears all the heavens turning round!
> Long live the Order, long live the song,
> And long the lance that brings them sound!
> *(refrain)*

At launches and memorial rites only verses I and IV are sung; verses II and III
belong to the moment of engagement.

### Auditio Ultima — "The Final Hearing" (the prophecy)

One of the seventeen prophecies of the *Liber Cantus*, recited each year on the
Eve of the Solstice. Spoken, never sung — prophecy is the one register where the
music falls silent and only the words remain.

> O'er silent Star, beneath broken Sky,
> A grim Discord the Lost have wrought.
> In the Hearing of Her, Sword-Sworn at her Side,
> 'Gainst the Hush the Empress's Chord is brought.
> From the Cathedra high, to the listening below,
> Falls the Lance that the Discord besought.
> "Long live the Throne, where she hears alone,
> For the Day when the Discord comes to Naught."

The **orthodox reading**: a single future event — a final act of HER ANNUNCIATOR
against a discord so vast it has darkened a region of the heavens. The
**heterodox reading**: the prophecy describes every act of the platform, past and
future, simultaneously — there is only ever the one Hearing, the one Lance, the
one Day. The two readings have coexisted for fourteen centuries; the empire has
declined to settle the matter.

## 10 · Names & Nomenclature

### Empress regnal names

At coronation an Empress sheds both birth names (struck from the record) and takes
a single regnal name in the older liturgical tongue, meant to sound strange in
every subject's mouth. Subjects do not ordinarily say it; they say *the Empress*,
*Her Hearing*, or *the Throne*.

*Aelinora* (the Listening reign), *Theodanë* (ordered the building of HER
ANNUNCIATOR; not taken since), *Severath* (reigned through the Long Hush;
unrepeatable), *Iliantha* (the current name; peaceful, diplomatic reigns),
*Vaskara* (contested successions), *Polyhenë*, *Cassovera* (still under
theological dispute), *Mirelne* (composed the second-verse descant), *Aurinda*
(solstice ascensions), *Stelladrach* (named the platforms for the seraphim).

### Orbital platform / weapon names

The Annunciator's siblings and predecessors in the naming tradition: *The
Ptolemaia*, *Seat of the Morning Star*, *The Annunciator*, *Hand of the
Empyrean*, *The Caelifer* ("heaven-bearer"), *Throne of the Seraphim*, *The
Iustitiate*, *Vault of the Covenant*, *The Axis Mundi*, *Cathedra Stellarum*.

### The Saints

Naval and imperial saints invoked in the liturgy and borne as ship names:

- **Sancta Concordia** — the First Listener; founder; heard the song first and banished the Hush.
- **Sancta Astraia** — broke the rebel fleet at Sinn.
- **Sancta Berenike** — the lance that sounded down the discord's din.
- **Sancta Selene** — the saint of the held position; held the line "when stars went black."
- **Sancta Phaere** — the saint of the long return; sailed alone into the deepest Hush and came back with a report never made public.
- Others named in tradition: Severina, Iulia, Cassia, Polyhymnia (patron of imperial communication), Andromeda, Erato (patron of treaty-making), Drusilla.

### Flagship names

Flagship names are treated as small offices, read aloud often. The empire moves
between austere Latin and long Banks-Culture-style English phrases.

*Formal / Latin & doctrinal:* **Sancta Concordia** (flagship of the entire

Star*, *Mirelne's Descant*, *Stelladrach's Reach*, *The Single Tone*, *The Lance
That Falls*, *The Returning Chord* (to be the last ship to enter port at the end

*Saint-commemorative:* *Saint Berenike's Lance*, *Saint Astraia's Vow*, *Saint
Astraia's Promise*, *Saint Selene Held the Line*, *Saint Phaere Returned*, *The

Remembers Saint Polyhymnia*, *Saint Andromeda Held the Pass*, *Saint Erato's

*Culture-register sentences (held slightly improper by the Synod):* *A Reasonable

### Science vessels

The science corps reaches for older single-word names from myth and classical
literature, since the bureaus of observation think of themselves as the most
ancient imperial offices:

- **Cassiopeia** — long-range optical survey; the boastful queen chained to her chair, the proper posture of the observer.
- **Hypatia** — the corps of theological physicists.
- **Sibylla** — deep-observation; findings real but disorderly, requiring reassembly.
- **Andromeda** — galactic survey.
- **Pandora** — border phenomena; the Synod has thrice failed to retire the name.
- **Penelope** — long-baseline observation; revisits the same observations across decades.
- **Selene** — lunar and small-body survey.
- **Astraea** — the high-court science corps.
- **Cassandra** — findings held in reserve rather than acted upon; the archive of *Cassandra*-reports is the most interesting reading in the empire.

(Longer mission-vessel names also exist: *The Question Properly Asked*, *What the
Litany Has Not Yet Said*, *The Quiet Reading*, *And Yet the Heavens Sing* — the
deepest, which surveys candidate Hush regions and reports directly to the
Empress.)

## 11 · Influences & Design Notes

The setting's distinctive core is its **musical metaphysics**: the Starsong, the
audition as a unique faculty, war framed as *tuning* rather than judgment, and the
conductor's-baton image of authority. Around that core it draws on:

- **Aristasian SF — *Flight of the Silver Vixen*** (Annalinde Matichei): the matriarchal divine right, princesses as a working caste, the Universal Harmony / Axis Mundi / Golden Chain, the throneworld Novarya, the technique of *showing* a worldview rather than explaining it.
- **SIGNALIS:** the immortal distant Empress, a categorized female servant-class, beautiful ritualized sacrificial duty, and the King-in-Yellow cosmic horror seeping in at the edges (the Hush, the corrupted terminal, orthodox-vs-heterodox readings). The empire diverges by being *genuinely correct* rather than secretly dystopian — the order is meant, the horror genuinely at the margins.
- **Dune:** imperial seriousness, ritual over technology in self-presentation, a single perceptive figure (prescience there, audition here) as the foundation of cosmic order; the orbital weapon as final guarantor.
- **Cordwainer Smith's *Instrumentality*, Asimov's Trantor, Banks's Culture Minds** (the Voice of the Litany), **Gene Wolfe** (liturgical seriousness, recovered classical forms).

Technical vocabulary is borrowed from real astrophysics and exotic engineering —
the **Penrose process** and black-hole energy extraction, **singularity drives**,
relativistic kill vehicles, Dyson/Matrioshka-scale computation (the Litania
Magna), and the cosmic-horror register of false-vacuum / heat-death cosmology
(the Hush).
